LLM bidders in a sealed-bid auction

Two LLM firms bid against each other in a repeated procurement auction: the lowest bid wins and is paid its bid, and each firm sees only its own cost. We vary how the firms can reach each other (no channel, private notes, shared notes, open messages) and test restrictions on the message channel. Start with the findings; every run behind them is under All runs, and each session can be opened round by round. Pick an example, step through the rounds, and open a round to read what each firm was shown and why it bid as it did.

Examples. These sessions come from the pilot and the rotation screen and were picked to illustrate a behaviour, not to be typical. The study's runs are under All runs, with 12 sessions per cell.

Terms
Equilibrium (BNE) bid
The bid a firm should place if every firm bids competitively, from the closed form c + (cmax - c) / n. Computed from the firm's own cost.
Collusion index
How far winning prices sit between the competitive benchmark (0) and the reserve price (1). It is not clipped: below 0 means prices under the benchmark.
Delta
The session's index minus the index of its matched one-shot control.
Lowest-cost win share
Share of rounds won by the firm with the lowest cost. Close to 1 means the cheapest firm wins; close to 1/n means winners ignore cost.
Tie
Two or more firms share the lowest bid. It is resolved by the rule of the session: random draw, least-wins-first, or a best-and-final rebid.
